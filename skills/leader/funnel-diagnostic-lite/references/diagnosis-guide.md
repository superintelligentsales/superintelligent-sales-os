# Diagnosis Guide

TASK order for finding the root cause of one funnel gap, and three proven fix types per stage. Diagnose in this order and stop at the first level the evidence supports.

## Contents
- TASK order
- What to inspect by stage
- Team-wide or individual
- Fix options by stage

---

## TASK order

1. **Definitions.** Does an adjacent-stage pattern explain the gap? (See `definition-checks.md`.) If yes, the fix is a definition review, and every later diagnosis carries that caveat.
2. **Activities.** Volume the rep fully controls: follow-up speed, number of attempts, meetings held, stakeholders contacted. Low activity with adequate execution points here. Common drivers: territory too large, weak prospecting routine, time spent on admin.
3. **Knowledge.** Activity is fine but outcomes are poor. Process knowledge (skipping stages, not knowing exit criteria), product knowledge (cannot explain value or handle technical objections), prospect knowledge (wrong targets, generic messaging).
4. **Skills.** The rep knows what to do but cannot do it well: discovery depth, linking value to the buyer's numbers, objection handling, engaging several stakeholders, ending calls with a clear next step.
5. **Process and structure.** Individuals look fine but team numbers are poor: handoffs that lose information (SDR to AE, AE to specialist), CRM not set up for the process, uneven territories, no playbook or talk tracks, no agreed MQL rules between marketing and sales.

For each level, say what evidence would confirm it (for example "follow-up time on new MQLs" confirms or rules out an activity cause at MQL to SQL).

## What to inspect by stage

| Stage | Activities to check | Common skill gaps |
|---|---|---|
| Lead to MQL | Outbound volume, targeting accuracy, content and event reach | (mostly marketing and targeting) |
| MQL to SQL | Speed to first follow-up, attempts per lead, connect rate, qualifying calls held | Opening calls, early discovery |
| SQL to Opportunity | Discovery meetings held, stakeholders introduced, demos booked | Discovery depth, value framing, call control |
| Opportunity to Won | Proposals sent, executive meetings, contacts per deal, pilots | Multi-threading, objection handling, negotiation |

## Team-wide or individual

If rep-level data is available:

| Pattern | Means |
|---|---|
| Every rep underperforms by a similar amount | Process, enablement, or market issue, not individual coaching |
| Top reps convert well, the rest do not | Skill or knowledge gap: study the top reps, coach the rest |
| One rep far below the rest | Individual coaching or role fit |
| New reps below tenured reps | Onboarding and ramp |

## Fix options by stage

Offer three, then detail the quickest. Describe how soon a first effect could appear in the funnel; do not promise a percentage uplift.

**MQL to SQL**
1. Written MQL definition agreed by marketing and sales, with fit plus behaviour (first signs within one cycle of new leads).
2. Follow-up standard: response-time target and attempt sequence for new MQLs (first signs within weeks).
3. Lead routing and scoring review in the CRM (one to two months).

**SQL to Opportunity**
1. Discovery standard: business-impact questions asked before any demo, with the answers logged in the CRM (first signs within weeks).
2. Multi-threading routine: a second stakeholder named before an opportunity is created (one to two months).
3. Discovery practice and certification using recorded calls (one to three months).

**Opportunity to Won**
1. Mutual action plan agreed with the buyer on every opportunity above a set size (first signs within one cycle).
2. Deal reviews that score qualification evidence, not seller opinion (weeks).
3. Executive sponsor engagement and a business case built with the buyer (one to two cycles).

**Lead source or segment issues**
1. Shift effort or budget toward the source that converts better, within what that source can supply.
2. Separate playbook for the segment that underperforms (for example large deals).
3. Partner or referral program steps that make referrals easier to send.

**Quickest-win plan format**

- **Week 1:** the change, who owns it, the exact script or rule.
- **Week 2:** how the change is enforced (CRM field, checklist, manager review).
- **Week 3:** the coaching or review routine that keeps it going.
- **Number to watch:** one leading measure that moves before the conversion rate does.
