# MEDDPICC Scoring Rubric

Eight elements, each scored with whole points only: 0, 1 or 2. Total out of 16. Never use half points in MEDDPICC; when the evidence sits between two levels, choose the lower one. All examples are fictional calibration quotes.

## Contents
- Scale
- Element calibration (1 to 8)
- Total bands
- Cost of doing nothing

---

## Scale

| Score | Label | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | None | No buyer evidence; the buyer never spoke to this element |
| 1 | Partial | Some buyer evidence, but incomplete or directional |
| 2 | Full | Complete buyer evidence covering every criterion; rare |

A 1 covers everything from a vague mention to good evidence with minor gaps. Reserve 2 for complete evidence.

## Element calibration

### 1. Metrics

The measurable outcomes the buyer will use to judge success.

| Score | Example |
|---|---|
| 0 | No success measures discussed. |
| 1 | "We want to save time on invoices." or "Cut processing from five days to one and errors below 2%." |
| 2 | Several metrics plus a return threshold and pilot success criteria: "Five days to one, errors under 2%, $180K a year in labor, payback under 18 months. If the pilot doesn't hit 2% in 90 days, we stop." |

The buyer must define the metrics. Seller-proposed metrics that the buyer agrees to score 0.

### 2. Economic Buyer

The person who controls budget and can say yes when others say no.

| Score | Example |
|---|---|
| 0 | Unknown. "Leadership will decide." |
| 1 | Named, or named and described as supportive, but not engaged on a supplied call with authority confirmed. |
| 2 | On the call, confirms budget and authority: "We have $250K allocated. If payback is under 18 months, I can approve it." |

### 3. Decision Criteria

How the buyer will choose, across three kinds of criteria: technical (integration, security), economic (price, return, terms), relationship (stability, support, references).

| Score | Example |
|---|---|
| 0 | No criteria discussed. |
| 1 | Some criteria in one or more categories, without priorities or deal-breakers. |
| 2 | All three kinds, prioritized, by stakeholder, with deal-breakers named, and a scorecard offered. |

"Easy to use" is not a criterion. "Our team will reject anything that adds steps to their workflow" is.

### 4. Decision Process

The internal steps, approvals, owners, and timeline.

| Score | Example |
|---|---|
| 0 | Not discussed. |
| 1 | "We'll review and get back to you." up to "Security review takes two weeks, Jane runs it, legal is one week." |
| 2 | A complete map with owners, durations, dependencies, and a start date the buyer is driving. |

Many meetings without moving through defined steps means a stalled deal. Ask which step the deal is on and what comes next.

### 5. Paper Process

The legal, procurement, and contract steps after the decision.

| Score | Example |
|---|---|
| 0 | Not discussed. |
| 1 | "Legal will review." up to the documents, the reviewer, and a typical duration. |
| 2 | The full path: documents, owners, timelines, the clauses legal usually redlines, how the purchase order is issued, and an introduction to the legal reviewer. |

Decision Process is who decides. Paper Process is how the contract gets signed.

### 6. Implicated Pain

Problems that are quantified and tied to wider consequences that create urgency.

| Level | What the buyer did |
|---|---|
| Identified | Named a problem (scores 0 on its own) |
| Quantified | Put a number on it (scores 1) |
| Implicated | Number plus consequences plus pressure (scores 2) |

| Score | Example |
|---|---|
| 0 | No problems discussed, or only named. |
| 1 | "Manual invoicing takes three people at 80% of their time, about $180K a year." |
| 2 | Several quantified costs, past failed fixes, personal toll, and executive pressure: "The CFO asked at last month's board meeting why this isn't solved." |

### 7. Champion

An internal advocate with power, who sells internally and has a personal stake.

| Score | Example |
|---|---|
| 0 | No advocate identified; just a contact. |
| 1 | Supportive or committed ("I'll introduce you to the CFO next week"), but not proven on all three criteria. |
| 2 | All three shown through action: briefed the economic buyer, shared their concerns, is presenting the business case internally. |

A helpful contact missing any criterion is a coach, not a champion.

### 8. Competition

Who and what the seller is up against: other vendors, doing nothing, building it in-house, or another project competing for the same budget.

| Score | Example |
|---|---|
| 0 | Never asked about alternatives. |
| 1 | "We're looking at a few options." up to named competitors with the buyer's view of them. |
| 2 | The full landscape, the buyer's view of each option, where the seller stands, and what the seller must prove to win. |

Knowing competitors exist is not a plan to beat them.

## Total bands

| Total | Label | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 4 | Needs Work | Major gaps; deal at high risk. Normal for an intro call. |
| 5 to 8 | Partial | Some elements qualified; significant gaps |
| 9 to 12 | Qualified | Solid; minor gaps to close |
| 13 to 16 | Strong | Deal well understood |

## Cost of doing nothing

When Metrics and Implicated Pain both score 2, add a short cost-of-doing-nothing line using only the buyer's own numbers: what the problem costs per month or quarter if nothing changes. If any number would have to be estimated, do not calculate it; state which number the seller should ask for.
