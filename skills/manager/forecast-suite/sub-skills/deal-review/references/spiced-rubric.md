# SPICED Scoring Rubric

Nine elements, each scored 0 to 2 in half points. Total out of 18. All examples are fictional buyer quotes used for calibration.

## Contents
- Scale
- Pain levels and event types
- Element calibration (1 to 9)
- Champion test
- Total bands

---

## Scale

| Score | Label | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | None | No buyer evidence |
| 0.5 | Minimal | A passing mention, one data point, no depth |
| 1.0 | Partial | Some evidence, key dimensions missing |
| 1.5 | Good | Strong evidence across several dimensions, minor gaps |
| 2.0 | Full | Complete evidence with direct quotes; rare |

## Pain levels and event types

**Pain levels** (Rational Pain and Emotional Pain):

| Level | What the buyer did | Score range |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Acknowledged | Named the problem | 0.5 to 1.0 |
| 2. Costed | Put time, money, or resources on it | 1.0 to 1.5 |
| 3. Personal | Said how it affects them or their team | 1.5 to 2.0 |

**Critical versus compelling events:**

| Type | Definition | Example | Cap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Critical | A date with a stated consequence if missed | "Board meeting October 10. If I don't have a solution, I'm in trouble." | 2.0 |
| Compelling | A preferred date with no stated consequence | "We'd like something by Q3." | 1.0 |

The test question is "What happens if this is not solved by then?" "Nothing really" means compelling.

## Element calibration

### 1. Situation

Facts about who they are: size, structure, systems, budget, strategic priorities. Not problems.

| Score | Example |
|---|---|
| 0.5 | "We're about 500 people in Chicago." |
| 1.0 | "We're a $50M manufacturer, 400 employees, three plants, running SAP." |
| 1.5 | "$120M distributor, 800 people, $15M IT budget, NetSuite and Salesforce. The board approved a digital program last quarter." |
| 2.0 | Full profile including budget, current system costs, leadership's plan, and what they evaluated before. |

Situation is what exists. Pain is what is wrong.

### 2. Rational Pain

Business problems with a measurable cost.

| Score | Example |
|---|---|
| 0.5 | "We have data silos." |
| 1.0 | "We lose deals because our quotes are slow." (consequence, no number) |
| 1.5 | "Quotes take five days. We lose about three deals a month to faster competitors, roughly $150K a quarter." |
| 2.0 | The buyer brings their own data: several metrics, lost revenue, hours spent, and how win rate changes with delay. |

Below 1.0 until the buyer quantifies it.

### 3. Emotional Pain

How the problem feels to the buyer and their team.

| Score | Example |
|---|---|
| 0.5 | "It's frustrating when deals slip." |
| 1.0 | "Honestly, it's frustrating. I have to apologize to customers myself." |
| 1.5 | "I'm exhausted by this. Every Monday I explain to leadership why we lost another one. Two of my best people are tired of looking bad." |
| 2.0 | Career stakes are explicit: "I was hired to fix this. If it's not turned around by Q3, I don't know if I'll still be here." |

Emotional pain often hides inside a rational statement. Score both when both are there.

### 4. Rational Impact

The measurable value of solving it, in the buyer's numbers.

| Score | Example |
|---|---|
| 0.5 | "We'd save time on quoting for sure." |
| 1.0 | "If we cut quote time in half, we'd probably close two or three more deals a month." |
| 1.5 | Several value buckets with the buyer's own calculations (hours back, extra deals, reduced overtime). |
| 2.0 | A complete business case the buyer owns: savings, revenue, cost removed, return and payback. |

The buyer must originate the numbers. A seller's ROI estimate that the buyer only agrees with scores 0.

### 5. Emotional Impact

What success would feel like: relief, recognition, career progress.

| Score | Example |
|---|---|
| 0.5 | "That would be great for the team." |
| 1.0 | "I'd finally stop firefighting and focus on strategy." |
| 1.5 | Team, customers, and the buyer's own standing with leadership all named. |
| 2.0 | Career-defining language and personal relief: "I'd be the one who fixed what three people before me couldn't. And I'd sleep better." |

Emotional impact often mirrors emotional pain: "keeps me up at night" becomes "I'd finally sleep."

### 6. Critical Event

A date with real consequences.

| Score | Example |
|---|---|
| 0.5 | "We'd like to modernize eventually." |
| 1.0 | "We're hoping to have something by Q3 for the new fiscal year." |
| 1.5 | "Our contract expires March 31. The CFO needs a recommendation by February 1 to make the board agenda." |
| 2.0 | Several converging deadlines, personal accountability, and a cost of delay: "Audit January 15, we failed last year and paid a $400K fine. Every month we wait costs about $50K." |

Enthusiasm is not urgency. If the buyer says "this isn't a let's-do-it-tomorrow thing", Critical Event is 0.

### 7. Decision Process

The steps, owners, and timeline to buy.

| Score | Example |
|---|---|
| 0.5 | "We'll look at a few options and decide." |
| 1.0 | "Demo with my team, then I take a recommendation to my VP, then procurement for anything over $50K." |
| 1.5 | Each step with an owner and a time frame, including parallel reviews. |
| 2.0 | A dated plan the buyer is already coordinating: meetings booked, reviewers told to expect it, business case drafted. |

### 8. Decision Criteria

The standards the buyer will use to choose.

| Score | Example |
|---|---|
| 0.5 | "Something easy to use that integrates with our systems." |
| 1.0 | "Salesforce integration, complex pricing rules, good reporting, within budget." |
| 1.5 | Criteria by stakeholder, prioritized, with deal-breakers named ("IT requires SOC 2 and SSO, non-negotiable"). |
| 2.0 | A documented, weighted scorecard, and the buyer says where the seller stands on it. |

### 9. Decision Committee

Who is involved, their influence, and how engaged they are.

| Score | Example |
|---|---|
| 0.5 | "Sarah is my main contact. Her boss will need to approve." |
| 1.0 | Key people named with roles and approval thresholds, but not engaged. |
| 1.5 | Several people engaged, the champion has acted (made an introduction), and competing internal priorities are known. |
| 2.0 | Full alignment: champion proven, economic buyer has committed directly, reviews cleared. |

Named is not engaged. Knowing the CFO's name caps this at 1.0. The CFO confirming budget earns 1.5 or more.

## Champion test

A contact is a champion only if they show all three, through actions:

| Criterion | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Power and influence | Can reach decision makers; credible internally |
| Sells internally | Advocates when the seller is not in the room |
| Personal stake | Gains something themselves (career, recognition, relief) |

An untested champion is not a champion. A helpful contact missing one of the three is a coach.

## Total bands

| Total | Label | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 5.5 | Needs Work | Major gaps; high risk. Normal for a first call. |
| 6 to 9.5 | Partial | Some elements qualified; significant gaps remain |
| 10 to 13.5 | Qualified | Solid discovery; minor gaps to close |
| 14 to 18 | Strong | Deal well understood |
