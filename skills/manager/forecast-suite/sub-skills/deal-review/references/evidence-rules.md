# Evidence Rules

These rules apply to both SPICED and MEDDPICC. They are what make a score trustworthy. Breaking them produces a story, not a review.

## The six rules

1. **Buyer statements only.** Score only what a buyer said. If no buyer said it, the score is 0.
2. **No inference.** If a claim needs an assumption about what the buyer "probably meant", the score is 0.
3. **Direct quotes required.** Every point awarded cites a verbatim buyer quote with a call label and timestamp. No quote, no points.
4. **Supplied calls only.** Seller notes, CRM fields, earlier calls that were not supplied, and outside research do not count unless a buyer confirms them on a supplied call. A buyer referring back ("as we discussed, it's about $150K a quarter") does count, because the buyer said it on this call.
5. **Agreement alone does not count.** When the seller says something and the buyer only agrees, the buyer added nothing. See the elaboration test.
6. **Conservative scoring.** Between two scores, choose the lower one. Top scores are rare and need evidence across every dimension of the element.

## The elaboration test

Did the buyer add substance (a new fact, number, name, date, or feeling), or only confirm what the seller said?

| Buyer response | Example | Counts? |
|---|---|---|
| Plain agreement | "Yeah." "Right." "Exactly." | No |
| Strong agreement | "Yes, that's exactly it." | No |
| Agreement plus detail | "Yes, and it's been that way for 18 months." | Yes |
| Agreement plus a number | "Absolutely, it costs us about $50K a quarter." | Yes |
| Agreement plus feeling | "Yeah... honestly it's exhausting." | Yes |

When a buyer only agrees, score the element on other evidence, and use that moment for the coachable moment: the seller led, the buyer followed, and the next call needs the buyer to say it in their own words.

## Choosing the quote

Collect every relevant buyer statement for an element before scoring. Then pick the one that:

- makes the score obvious to a stranger who reads only the quote;
- a skeptic could not reasonably read as supporting a different score;
- for Emotional Pain and Emotional Impact, carries the buyer's tone ("honestly", "exhausted", "keeps me up at night", "my credibility", "I'd finally sleep"), not only the facts.

The first quote found is rarely the strongest one.

## Quote format

```
> "Exact words from the transcript" (Buyer, Call 1, 12:34)
```

- Use the speaker's name and title when it matters: `(Jane Smith, CFO, Call 2, 08:10)`.
- Seller quotes may appear for context, labeled `(Seller, ...)`, and never earn points.
- Quote verbatim. Keep filler words and imperfect grammar. Do not clean up or paraphrase.
- If the transcript has no timestamps, cite the call and a rough position (`Call 1, near the end`) and say once that timestamps were missing.

## Common scoring errors

| Error | Wrong | Right |
|---|---|---|
| Crediting the seller | Seller: "So you're losing about $100K a quarter?" Buyer: "Yeah." Scored as quantified pain. | 0. The buyer added nothing. |
| Situation scored as pain | "We have three legacy systems" scored as pain. | That is a fact about the situation. Pain is "those systems cost us 10 hours of manual work a week." |
| Breadth over depth | Five pains mentioned in passing scored as full pain. | One deeply quantified pain beats five vague ones. |
| Missing embedded emotion | "We lose $50K a quarter and I have to explain it to the board" scored only as rational pain. | The second half is emotional pain (personal accountability). Score both. |
| Identified treated as engaged | "The CFO will need to approve" scored high on the committee or economic buyer. | Named but not engaged caps the element (1.0 in SPICED, 1 in MEDDPICC). |
| Untested champion | A friendly, enthusiastic contact scored as a champion. | Credit a champion only for actions taken. |
| Compelling treated as critical | "We'd like this by Q3" scored as a critical event. | No consequence means compelling, 1.0 at most in SPICED. |
| "As soon as possible" as a process | "We'd like to close quickly" scored as decision process. | No steps, owners, or dates means 0. |
| Status quo ignored | No vendor named, so Competition scored 0 as "no competition". | Doing nothing and building it in-house are competition. Score what the buyer said about them. |
| Market sizing as buyer impact | "It's a $500M market" scored as rational impact. | Impact is what this buyer gains from this solution, in their numbers. |

## Edge cases

- **Several buyers on the call.** Score on the strongest evidence from any buyer, and say who provided it.
- **Short calls (under 15 minutes).** Expect low scores. Score what was discussed and note the length.
- **Long calls (over 60 minutes).** Quality still beats quantity. Watch for positions that changed during the call.
- **Later-stage calls.** Discovery calls show more pain and situation; demos show criteria and objections; negotiations show paper process, competition, and economic buyer. Judge gaps against the call type, but still score them.
- **Buyer uses the seller's materials.** Reading back the seller's ROI figure does not count. Validating it does: "Your model showed $200K, and that matches our internal estimate."
- **Contradictions.** Show both quotes with timestamps and score the weaker position. The contradiction is a finding.

## Depth checks

Before finalizing any element, scan the whole transcript again for it.

1. **Asides carry evidence.** "Oh, by the way, our CFO mentioned budget might be tight next quarter" is economic buyer and budget evidence in a throwaway line.
2. **Stories carry pain and impact.** A story about a failed project or a frustrated team holds who was affected, what happened, and how long. Capture the details, not just the conclusion.
3. **Implicit triggers are still triggers.** "We just got a new CRO" or "we're reorganizing" are events even when the buyer does not frame them that way. Tag them and ask about consequences next call.
4. **Tag every number.** Headcount, revenue, timelines, percentages: capture each with its exact value and timestamp.
5. **Answer "so what?"** Every element's note must say what the evidence means for how to sell this deal, not just what was observed.
