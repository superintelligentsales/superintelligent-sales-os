# MEDDPICC Scoring Rubric: Half-Point Levels

Each element scores 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, or 2. Total is out of 16. Every point requires a
buyer-originated quote. When evidence falls between two levels, choose the lower one.
A score of 2 is rare.

The example statements below are fictional and illustrate the level, not a script.

## Metrics: the buyer's own measures of success

| Score | Evidence required | Example buyer statement |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | No success measure from the buyer. A metric the seller proposed and the buyer accepted scores 0. | (none) |
| 0.5 | A general benefit with no measure. | "It would help us be more efficient." |
| 1 | A clear direction with no number. | "We want less time spent on invoice processing." |
| 1.5 | Specific measures with targets; ROI threshold or proof criteria missing. | "We need processing down from five days to one and errors under 2 percent." |
| 2 | Several measures, a return threshold, and what must be proven to proceed. | "Five days to one, errors under 2 percent, payback inside 18 months. If the pilot misses the error target in 90 days, we stop." |

## Economic Buyer: the person who can approve the spend

| Score | Evidence required | Example buyer statement |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | No one identified, or only "leadership." | "Leadership will decide." |
| 0.5 | A role, no name. | "My boss would need to approve." |
| 1 | Named, with authority stated; not engaged. | "Our CFO, Jane, signs anything over 100 thousand." |
| 1.5 | Named and engaged; authority not yet confirmed by that person. | "I walked Jane through it last week and she is supportive." |
| 2 | The economic buyer confirms budget and authority directly. | Jane: "We have the budget. If payback is under 18 months, I can approve it." |

## Decision Criteria: how they will choose

Criteria fall into three groups: technical (integration, security), economic (price,
payback, terms), and relationship (references, support, vendor stability).

| Score | Evidence required | Example buyer statement |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | No criteria from the buyer. | (none) |
| 0.5 | Generic requirements. | "It needs to work well and be affordable." |
| 1 | Specific criteria in one or two groups, no priority. | "It has to integrate with our TMS and pass security review." |
| 1.5 | Criteria across two or more groups with deal-breakers named. | "TMS integration and SOC 2 are must-haves. Payback under 18 months is required." |
| 2 | All groups, priorities, deal-breakers, and who cares about what. | "Integration is a deal-breaker for me; the CFO cares most about risk. I will send you our scorecard." |

## Decision Process: the steps to a decision

| Score | Evidence required | Example buyer statement |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Not discussed. | (none) |
| 0.5 | Generic description. | "We will review internally." |
| 1 | Key steps, missing owners or timing. | "It goes to IT for security, then to the CFO." |
| 1.5 | Steps with owners and timing; no full map or shared plan. | "Security review takes two weeks and Sam runs it. Then the CFO." |
| 2 | Full map with owners, timing, dependencies, and a shared plan. | "Security with Sam starts Monday, two weeks. CFO approval three days after. If we start Monday we can decide in three weeks." |

## Paper Process: how the contract gets signed

| Score | Evidence required | Example buyer statement |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Not discussed. | (none) |
| 0.5 | Legal or procurement mentioned, no specifics. | "Legal will need to look at it." |
| 1 | Documents or steps named. | "We need an MSA and a DPA, then procurement issues a PO." |
| 1.5 | Documents, owners, and timing; signatory or redline expectations missing. | "Tom in legal reviews contracts and usually takes a week." |
| 2 | Full process with owners, timing, signatory, likely redlines, and an offer to coordinate. | "Tom will redline liability caps. The CFO signs after legal. PO in three to five days. I will introduce you to Tom." |

## Implicated Pain: problems with consequences

Pain moves through three levels: identified (named), quantified (a number attached), and
implicated (connected to consequences, other stakeholders, or pressure from leadership).

| Score | Evidence required | Example buyer statement |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | No problem discussed. | (none) |
| 0.5 | Problem named, not explored. | "Our process is inefficient." |
| 1 | Quantified in one dimension. | "It takes the team 40 hours a week." |
| 1.5 | Quantified in several dimensions; no consequence or urgency. | "Three people at full capacity, about 180 thousand a year, plus 15 thousand in late fees." |
| 2 | Quantified, with consequences and pressure. | "...and the CFO asked at the last board meeting why this is still not fixed." |

## Champion: an advocate who acts

A champion has three traits: power or access to the economic buyer, advocacy when the
seller is not present, and a personal stake. Each trait is shown through action, not stated.

| Score | Evidence required | Example buyer statement |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | No advocate identified. | (none) |
| 0.5 | Seems supportive; nothing validated. | "This looks great." |
| 1 | One trait stated, untested. | "This is my priority and I will help." |
| 1.5 | Two or more traits, at least one shown through action; power not yet proven. | "I set up time with our CFO for Thursday. She will ask about risk first." |
| 2 | All three traits shown through action. | "I shared your model with the CFO before the meeting. She is on board. I present to my team tomorrow." |

## Competition: every alternative to buying from you

Alternatives include other vendors, the status quo, an internal build, and other projects
competing for the same budget.

| Score | Evidence required | Example buyer statement |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Not discussed. | (none) |
| 0.5 | Alternatives exist, unnamed. | "We are looking at a few options." |
| 1 | Alternatives named; the buyer's view of them unclear. | "We are also talking to one other vendor, or we keep patching our spreadsheet." |
| 1.5 | The buyer's view of each alternative is clear; your position is not. | "They are cheaper, but we hear their rollout is slow." |
| 2 | Full picture, the buyer's view of each, your position, and what you must prove. | "You are ahead on integration. Prove the payback and it is yours." |

## Common scoring errors

1. Crediting the seller: the seller says it, the buyer says "yeah." Score 0.
2. Treating a named economic buyer as engaged. Named is 1 at most.
3. Treating a friendly contact as a champion before any action.
4. Scoring quantified pain as implicated without a consequence.
5. Using one quote for both Decision Process and Paper Process.
6. Counting meetings as progress through the Decision Process.
7. Scoring Competition 0 because no vendor was named, when the status quo was discussed.
8. Using values other than 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, and 2.
