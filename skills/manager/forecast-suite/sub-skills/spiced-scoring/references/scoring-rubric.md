# SPICED Scoring Rubric: Half-Point Levels

Nine elements, each scored 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, or 2. Total is out of 18. Every point requires
a buyer-originated quote. When evidence falls between two levels, choose the lower one.
A score of 2 is rare.

The example statements below are fictional and illustrate the level, not a script.

## Situation: the facts of who they are

Size, locations, systems, team structure, budget, and strategic plans. Facts, not problems.

| Score | Evidence required | Example buyer statement |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | No facts from the buyer. | (none) |
| 0.5 | A single fact. | "We're about 500 people." |
| 1 | A basic profile; budget, leadership, or strategy missing. | "We're 400 people across three sites, all on one ERP." |
| 1.5 | Most dimensions covered, including budget or a strategic plan. | "800 people, a 15 million IT budget, and the board approved a modernization plan last quarter." |
| 2 | Complete context, including what they tried before. | "...and we evaluated another vendor last year and stopped at security review." |

## Rational Pain: the measurable cost of the problem

| Score | Evidence required | Example buyer statement |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | No problem from the buyer. | (none) |
| 0.5 | Problem named, no cost. | "We have data silos." |
| 1 | Problem with a consequence or one number. | "We lose deals because quotes take too long." |
| 1.5 | Cost quantified in more than one dimension. | "Quotes take five days and we lose about three deals a month to faster vendors." |
| 2 | The buyer owns the data and gives a full cost picture. | "I pulled it last week: eleven lost deals this quarter, and sales spends twelve hours a week chasing approvals." |

## Emotional Pain: how the problem feels

| Score | Evidence required | Example buyer statement |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | No feeling expressed. | (none) |
| 0.5 | Mild frustration, not explored. | "It's frustrating when deals slip." |
| 1 | Clear personal frustration or involvement. | "I'm the one who apologizes to customers when we're slow." |
| 1.5 | Personal strain plus team or reputation effects. | "I'm worn out explaining this every Monday, and my best people are tired of looking bad." |
| 2 | Career or standing at stake, stated by the buyer. | "I was hired to fix this. If it isn't fixed by spring, I don't know that I'm still here." |

## Rational Impact: the measurable gain from solving it

| Score | Evidence required | Example buyer statement |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | No gain from the buyer. A seller's estimate the buyer accepts scores 0. | (none) |
| 0.5 | Direction, no number. | "We'd save time for sure." |
| 1 | One measure or a basic estimate. | "If we halved quote time we'd close two or three more deals a month." |
| 1.5 | Several measures with the buyer's own math. | "Five days to one gives us ten hours a week back, and three more deals a month." |
| 2 | A complete business case the buyer owns. | "I built the case: time, revenue, and a retired workaround. Payback in about two months." |

## Emotional Impact: what success would mean to the buyer

| Score | Evidence required | Example buyer statement |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Nothing said. | (none) |
| 0.5 | A vague positive. | "That would be great for the team." |
| 1 | Personal relief stated. | "I could stop firefighting and work on strategy." |
| 1.5 | Personal, team, and customer benefits. | "My team would enjoy work again and I'd have good news for leadership." |
| 2 | A career-defining or recognized win. | "I'd be the one who fixed what three people before me couldn't." |

## Critical Event: a date with a consequence

A critical event is a date with a stated consequence if it is missed. A compelling event
is a preferred date with no consequence; it caps at 1. "As soon as possible" is neither.
An exploratory conversation scores 0. The test question: "What happens if this is not in
place by that date?"

| Score | Evidence required | Example buyer statement |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | No date, "as soon as possible," or the buyer says it is exploratory. | "We're just exploring." |
| 0.5 | A wish with no timing. | "We'd like to modernize eventually." |
| 1 | A preferred date, no consequence. | "We'd like something in place by the new fiscal year." |
| 1.5 | A specific date with an approval deadline or consequence. | "Our contract ends March 31, and the CFO needs a recommendation by February 1 for the board." |
| 2 | Several converging dates, consequences, and personal accountability. | "Board on October 10. Audit in January; we were fined last year. The CFO says this one is mine." |

## Decision Process: the steps to a decision

| Score | Evidence required | Example buyer statement |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Not discussed. | (none) |
| 0.5 | Generic description. | "We'll evaluate a few options." |
| 1 | Steps named, timing vague. | "I'd take it to our COO, then procurement." |
| 1.5 | Each step with timing and owner. | "IT review next week, I present at the leadership meeting on the 15th, procurement takes three weeks." |
| 2 | Full timeline with slots reserved and a shared plan. | "Leadership slot is booked for the 22nd. Procurement starts the 23rd. I've drafted the business case." |

## Decision Criteria: what they will judge on

| Score | Evidence required | Example buyer statement |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Not discussed. | (none) |
| 0.5 | Generic needs. | "It needs to be easy and integrate." |
| 1 | Specific criteria, some structure. | "It has to work on phones and connect to our accounting system." |
| 1.5 | Criteria by stakeholder, with deal-breakers. | "Speed matters most to me; IT requires single sign-on, no exceptions." |
| 2 | A weighted scorecard with your position on it. | "We built a scorecard. Speed is 30 percent. You lead on what I care about most." |

## Decision Committee: who is involved and how engaged

Identifying a person is not engaging them. A champion is shown through action:
introductions made, inside information shared, internal advocacy.

| Score | Evidence required | Example buyer statement |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | No one beyond the contact. | (none) |
| 0.5 | An unnamed approver. | "My boss would need to sign off." |
| 1 | Approver named with authority; not engaged. | "Ray, our COO, approves anything over 25 thousand." |
| 1.5 | Several people engaged; the champion has acted. | "I introduced you to Ray last week and he's supportive. IT confirmed security works." |
| 2 | Full alignment, the approver committed, obstacles cleared. | "Ray told you directly this is his first-quarter priority, and IT signed off in writing." |

## Common scoring errors

1. Crediting the seller: the seller says it, the buyer says "right." Score 0.
2. Scoring a fact as pain. "We run on spreadsheets" is Situation.
3. Rewarding many shallow pains over one quantified pain.
4. Missing emotion inside a factual statement. "And I have to explain it to the board" is emotional pain.
5. Treating a named approver as engaged.
6. Treating a preferred date as a critical event.
7. Using values other than 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, and 2.
