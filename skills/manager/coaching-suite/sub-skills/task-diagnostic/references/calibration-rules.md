# Internal calibration rules

These are routing heuristics for the diagnostic, not user-facing personnel standards.
Keep their cut-offs out of manager and rep outputs. Show the actual situation in plain
language: "with this few deals a year," counts, uncertainty, and the next useful action.

## Volume and count gates

1. Align projected bookings and remaining target within each quota component. A ratio
   below 90% is Short: volume first. From 90% to below 100% is Close: state the deal gap
   and need for more volume before conversion. At least 100% is Enough for the next gate,
   not guaranteed attainment. Missing inputs or a nonpositive target require clarification.
2. Fewer than 30 projected annual wins per channel: counts first, rates are assumptions.
   Fewer than 12: one observable behavior, no numeric rate target or promised annual lift.
3. Six or fewer new-logo wins needed: deal-level first. Inspect eligible opportunities
   before general activity volume. Expansion deficits never imply new-logo prospecting.
4. Fewer than one projected win per month: use activities that produce the next deal,
   never fractional weekly deal quotas. Annualize only when the period supports it;
   otherwise state that annual volume is unknown. Do not annualize a seasonal burst blindly.

Apply the earliest unresolved gate. Small-book deal-level inspection refines the volume
recommendation; it does not permit skipping volume for skill training.

## Measured-history sufficiency

Apply only to decided deals, with consistent outcome definitions and cohort boundaries:

| Decided deals | Internal method label | Permitted interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Under 30 | Anecdote | Counts, direct observations; no reported win-rate percentage. |
| 30–99 | Directional | Descriptive rate with denominator and uncertainty. |
| 100–384 | Reportable | Report descriptive comparisons with cohort limitations. |
| 385–949 | Statistical | Statistical analysis may be appropriate if its assumptions hold. |
| 950 or more | Attribution-grade | Large-sample analysis is possible; causal attribution still requires a valid design. |

These labels are not guarantees of precision, significance, representativeness, or cause.
Use the less reliable cohort to limit a comparison; a large peer group cannot rescue a
small rep sample. Do not use projected deal counts as sample size. No automatic confidence
score follows from this table. Mixed cohorts, missing outcomes, dependence, and selection
bias can make even a large sample unsuitable.

## Benchmarks

Use matched peers, then matched rep history, then verified primary industry sources.
Compare win rate to win rate. Do not invent stage-rate or end-to-end benchmarks by deal
size. For a verified matched industry range, more than two percentage points below the
lower end is below; more than two points above the upper end is above. Otherwise describe
it as within or near the range. This is a descriptive routing rule, not a significance
test. Do not apply it to unmatched cohorts or small anecdotal samples.

## Coverage and probability

Break-even coverage is 1 / win rate. At 25%, that is 4x, an expected-value relationship,
not safe coverage. Pipeline value required at break-even is remaining target / win rate
only under compatible value and win-rate assumptions. Opportunity-count coverage and
value coverage are different measures; state the chosen definition without resolving the
method's open policy question. Unequal deal values can invalidate a count-rate value model.

The "about 55% at break-even" illustration depends on deal count and a probability model.
For example, with 100 independent equal-value opportunities, each with a 10% win chance,
the probability of at least 10 wins is approximately 54.87%. With 10 such opportunities,
the probability of at least one is 1 − 0.9^10 = 65.13%. Neither is universal. Do not
estimate probabilities without support for independence, comparable probabilities, timing,
and deal values. Use transparent scenarios when those assumptions are unavailable.

## Unresolved method choices

Keep open: guided self-discovery versus direct instruction; count versus value coverage;
the sales-cycle start event; and whether a 120% "covered" band should be adopted. Do not
activate that band. Record run-specific definitions supplied by the manager as local
choices, not as policy or new context schema fields.
