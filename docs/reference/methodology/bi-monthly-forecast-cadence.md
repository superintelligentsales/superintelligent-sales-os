# Bi-Monthly Forecast Cadence

> Methodology reference. Loaded by `manager/bi-monthly-forecast-review`, `manager/mid-month-checkpoint`, `manager/deal-confidence`, and `manager/qbr-prep`.

## When to use this

Whenever a sales organization is establishing or reforming its forecast cadence. The framework defines *when* forecast inspection happens; `forecast-triangulation-method.md` defines *how* the forecast is produced inside that cadence.

## The core thesis (contrarian)

The standard practice across most B2B sales orgs is the weekly forecast call. The standard practice is wrong for most teams.

Weekly forecast inspection produces diminishing returns because deals don't move stages every week. Typical stage movement takes 12–15 days. Inspecting the same deal seven days after the last inspection produces no new information ~70% of the time — which trains reps to fabricate "updates" to fill the void. The forecast call degrades into theater.

The right cadence for most teams is **bi-monthly**: a comprehensive Beginning-of-Month review and a Mid-Month checkpoint. The hours recovered from the cancelled weekly forecast call get reinvested into coaching and deal strategy, where the marginal hour is far higher leverage.

## Why weekly forecast calls fail

Three structural problems:

1. **Stage movement is slower than the inspection cadence.** A weekly forecast call inspects the same deal multiple times before any real change has occurred. Reps perform progress because they have nothing real to report.
2. **Repetition breeds storytelling.** When there's nothing factual to surface, reps narrate. The meeting becomes a debrief of customer politeness signals rather than evidence-based qualification.
3. **Time waste compounds.** A 30-minute weekly forecast call across 8 reps consumes ~26 hours of manager time per quarter on a low-information activity. That same 26 hours, redirected to coaching, generates measurable performance improvement (forecast accuracy, win rate, sales cycle).

The math favors coaching. Every weekly forecast call kept is roughly an hour of coaching not delivered.

## The bi-monthly structure

### Beginning of Month: Comprehensive Review (90 minutes)

The anchor meeting of the cadence. Output is a fully triangulated forecast with confidence band.

| Activity | Duration |
|---|---|
| Pull complete pipeline view | Pre-work |
| Inspect every deal expected to close in the period | 60 min (~8 min per deal across ~10 deals) |
| Apply SPICED rigor to each | embedded |
| Establish forecast commitment for the period (Commit / Best Case / Pipeline) | 15 min |
| Identify at-risk deals and resource needs | 15 min |

Pre-work is non-negotiable. 24 hours before the meeting, the rep submits in CRM: updated SPICED scorecard with evidence, stakeholder map with engagement status, specific next step with date, forecast category, confidence assessment with rationale.

### Mid-Month: Adjustment Checkpoint (45 minutes)

Half the BOM duration, focused only on what has changed.

| Activity | Duration |
|---|---|
| Review changes since BOM | 15 min |
| Surface deals that have slipped or accelerated | 15 min |
| Adjust forecast based on new information | 10 min |
| Reallocate resources to highest-leverage deals | 5 min |

### The Month-3 Problem (the defining business case)

The signature failure mode of weekly forecast cadences is the Month-3 surprise. Quarter month 1 looks fine. Month 2 looks fine. Month 3 reveals a $1M+ gap that was actually opening up in month 2 — but the manager was inspecting end-of-quarter commit using weekly storytelling, not evidence-based mid-month inspection, so the slip surfaced too late to recover.

The Mid-Month checkpoint is specifically designed to surface this gap **4 weeks earlier** than a weekly cadence does — when there is still time to accelerate at-risk deals, redirect resources, or set leadership expectations honestly. This single inspection moment recovers more forecast credibility than any other intervention in the manager's calendar.

### End-of-Month → Beginning-of-Next-Month

Rolled into the next BOM review:

- Results vs. forecast comparison
- Forecast accuracy analysis per rep
- Lessons captured and fed into next period

## What replaces the weekly forecast call

The recovered weekly time gets reinvested in three places:

1. **Pipeline scrubs** — embedded in 1:1s. Data hygiene happens during the rep's coaching hour, not in a standalone forum.
2. **Deep Dive Deal Reviews** — collaborative strategy on 2–3 deals per week (Friday Week A focus session).
3. **Skill development sessions** — Friday Week B focus session, coaching workshops, role play.

This is not a reduction in deal inspection. It is a reallocation of inspection from a low-leverage forum (weekly forecast call) to higher-leverage forums (1:1, deal review, skill workshop).

## When weekly forecast calls *are* appropriate

The bi-monthly cadence is the default. The exceptions:

| Condition | Why weekly is justified |
|---|---|
| Last month of quarter | Commit is being finalized; the inspection density matters |
| Crisis recovery | Two consecutive missed forecasts; trust is broken; weekly inspection rebuilds credibility |
| New manager (first 90 days) | Manager is establishing baseline pattern recognition; needs higher inspection frequency |
| New product/segment launch | No historical data; inspection is the only signal source |

In all four cases, the weekly cadence is temporary. The default state is bi-monthly.

## Forecast accuracy as the proof point

The bi-monthly cadence, run rigorously alongside the triangulation method, drives the headline business outcome Dana commits to with new clients: **15–20% improvement in forecast accuracy within one quarter.** Forecast accuracy is the single best leading indicator of overall manager capability — it compounds the manager's credibility with leadership, which compounds the resources and autonomy they earn, which compounds team performance.

A manager who forecasts within ±10% over four quarters has, by definition, inspected deals rigorously, coached reps to qualify well, held the team accountable to evidence, and built a culture of honesty. The bi-monthly cadence is what makes all four of those possible without consuming the calendar.

Per-rep forecast accuracy should be tracked, published monthly, and trended over quarters. What gets measured gets managed; what gets published builds peer accountability.

## Common objections and how to handle them

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "Leadership wants weekly numbers" | Provide weekly numbers via dashboard (CRM-pulled, low effort). The *meeting* is bi-monthly; the *reporting* can be weekly. |
| "How will I know if a deal is slipping?" | The mid-month checkpoint catches it. If a deal slips between BOM and Mid-Month, the rep escalates in 1:1, not in a forecast call. |
| "Reps need the discipline of weekly accountability" | Weekly accountability happens in the 1:1, where it should happen. Forecast calls are not the appropriate forum for general accountability. |
| "We always do weekly" | "Always" is not a justification. Audit the last quarter's weekly forecast calls and ask: what new information did each one surface? If the answer is "very little 70% of the time," the data has answered the question. |

## Implementation sequencing

For teams transitioning from weekly to bi-monthly:

1. **Month 1:** Run BOM (90 min) and Mid-Month (45 min) in addition to existing weekly calls. Compare information yield.
2. **Month 2:** Drop weekly calls. Reps and manager use the recovered time for coaching and pipeline scrubs in 1:1s.
3. **Month 3:** Measure. Forecast accuracy should hold or improve. Coaching session count should rise. Reactive escalations should decrease.

If forecast accuracy degrades in month 3, the issue is almost never the cadence — it is CRM hygiene, SPICED discipline, or a specific rep with a personal probability multiplier issue. Fix those upstream causes; do not revert the cadence.
