# Managing to Metrics: Library

> Methodology reference. Loaded by `manager/management-analytics`, `manager/one-on-one-prep`, `manager/bi-monthly-forecast-review`, `manager/friday-deal-review`, and any Manager Layer skill that surfaces or coaches against a specific leading indicator.

## When to use this

Whenever a manager identifies a performance gap and needs to drill down into the metric beneath the metric. Quota attainment is a lagging indicator; it cannot be coached directly. The metrics in this library are the leading and intermediate indicators that *can* be coached. Each card maps a metric to its diagnostic questions, its TASK-level root cause, and the Champion's Code element that operationalizes the fix.

## The framework

The library implements the **Managing-to-Metrics** principle: results are downstream of activities; activities are downstream of skills and knowledge. Coaching to the result ("close more deals") is useless. Coaching to the metric beneath the result ("increase your discovery-to-demo conversion from 35% to 50% by tightening pain quantification") is actionable.

Every metric card follows the same structure:

- **Definition** — what the metric measures
- **What it means** — why it matters and what it tells you
- **Diagnostic questions** — what to inspect when the metric is off
- **TASK level** — Target / Activity / Skill / Knowledge — where the root cause typically lives
- **Champion's Code element** — which cultural element operationalizes improvement

Use the cards in two modes: **diagnostic** (a metric is off — drill down to root cause) and **proactive** (build a development plan around a target metric improvement).

## Pipeline metrics

### 1. Pipeline Volume

**Definition.** The total number of opportunities in the rep's active pipeline.

**What it means.** With a 10% win probability and 10 independent comparable opportunities, the chance of at least one close is 1 − 0.9^10, approximately 65%; one expected close is not a guaranteed close. Volume is the foundation; everything else is leverage on top of it.

**Diagnostic questions.** Is calendar time blocked for prospecting? Do they understand which accounts to target (ICP fit)? Are they hitting minimum dial and email activity? Are emails personalized or template-spray? Do they have an account plan?

**TASK level.** Activity (A) — usually a volume gap in prospecting touches. Sometimes Knowledge (K) — the rep doesn't know which accounts qualify as ICP.

**Champion's Code element.** Element 2 (Know Your Numbers) for visibility; Element 4 (Practice) for prospecting skill build.

### 2. Pipeline Value

**Definition.** The monetary value of the active pipeline in dollars.

**What it means.** Break-even coverage is 1 ÷ win rate; required pipeline value is remaining revenue goal ÷ win rate under compatible value assumptions. Break-even is expected-value coverage, not safety: an illustrative model of 100 independent equal-value opportunities at a 10% win probability gives about a 55% chance of reaching 10 wins; the probability depends on the deal distribution. Two reps with identical pipeline volume can have wildly different value depending on deal-size discipline.

**Diagnostic questions.** What industries make up the pipeline? Are they targeting decision-makers or end users? Are they recommending the full product suite or a narrow piece? What's the outbound lead rating — A's and B's, or C's filling space?

**TASK level.** Skill (S) — recommendation strength and pricing discipline. Sometimes Activity (A) — wrong-tier accounts being worked.

**Champion's Code element.** Element 3 (Coaching) on value selling and recommendations.

### 3. Pipeline Coverage

**Definition.** Total pipeline value divided by quota for the period. Inverse of close rate (1-in-4 close rate → 4x coverage needed).

**What it means.** A measurement of whether the rep has *enough* in flight to hit their number, accounting for their actual close rate and deal velocity. Standard guidance: rolling 3-5x, adjusted for sales cycle length.

**Diagnostic questions.** What's the rep's actual close rate over the last four quarters? What's the typical sales cycle? Is coverage concentrated in one or two large deals (high-variance risk)? What's the prospecting strategy — joint with SDR, ABM, manager-supported?

**TASK level.** Activity (A) at the source — pipeline build. Sometimes Target (T) — coverage ratio assumptions are wrong for this rep's segment.

**Champion's Code element.** Element 2 (Know Your Numbers) — this is the headline indicator reps should track weekly.

### 4. Opportunity Recency

**Definition.** Days since last meaningful activity on the opportunity.

**What it means.** Deals do not drift, regress, or go dark on their own — those outcomes happen because the rep failed to align process with the prospect. Recency is the early-warning system.

**Diagnostic questions.** What was the last meeting and the agreed next step? Is there a joint evaluation plan? Has the rep made proactive outreach since? Are they triangulating across stakeholders or relying on one champion?

**TASK level.** Skill (S) — deal management discipline.

**Champion's Code element.** Element 3 (Coaching) on deal management; Element 5 (Collaboration) for peer review of stalled deals.

### 5. Opportunity Age / Velocity

**Definition.** Total time the opportunity has been worked, measured against the standard sales cycle benchmark.

**What it means.** Time kills deals. Deals beyond standard cycle length without proportional progress are at risk. Inspect not only total age, but time in stage and stages remaining.

**Diagnostic questions.** Has the deal slipped past the standard win timeframe? Is it stalled in a late stage (high risk) or moving steadily through stages? Have they identified a critical event? Is the decision-making process documented and validated?

**TASK level.** Skill (S) — discovery and qualification rigor at the front end determines velocity.

**Champion's Code element.** Element 3 (Coaching) on early-stage rigor; Element 6 (Continuous Learning) — slow deals are the primary teaching material.

## Activity metrics

### 6. Activity — Dials

**Definition.** Number of dials made on a daily or weekly basis.

**What it means.** Whether prospecting, pipeline management, or upsell, talking to humans is the foundation of revenue work.

**Diagnostic questions.** Is calendar time actually blocked for dial blocks, and is the rep adhering? Are lead lists prepped before each block? Do they have prep time scheduled? Are they multi-threading (3-5 contacts per account)?

**TASK level.** Activity (A) — volume directly. Sometimes Skill (S) — low confidence depresses dial count.

**Champion's Code element.** Element 2 (Know Your Numbers) for visibility; Element 7 (Celebration) — dials are a celebrate-able behavior.

### 7. Activity — Emails

**Definition.** Number of emails sent on a daily or weekly basis.

**What it means.** Email is a primary channel and a measurable activity. Volume without quality is a domain-burning strategy.

**Diagnostic questions.** Which cadences are they using? Are emails personalized or template-spray? What's their open rate, reply rate, click rate? Are they following the RRR framework? Subject lines short and curiosity-driven?

**TASK level.** Activity (A) for volume; Skill (S) for quality.

**Champion's Code element.** Element 4 (Practice) — email writing is a high-leverage practice skill.

### 8. Activity Volume (meetings)

**Definition.** Total meetings by type — discovery, demo, negotiation, etc.

**What it means.** A measurement of meaningful work actually getting done, and a proxy for whether deals are being actively progressed. Also: meetings-per-booking is a measure of efficiency — if one rep closes in 4 meetings and another takes 8, the first rep is materially more effective.

**Diagnostic questions.** Does meeting volume align with where the rep should be in their deals? Are meetings being properly prepped (agendas sent, ACE applied)? Is each meeting moving to a clear next step?

**TASK level.** Activity (A) for the count; Skill (S) for meetings-per-booking efficiency.

**Champion's Code element.** Element 4 (Practice) for meeting quality; Element 6 (Continuous Learning) for meetings-per-booking analysis.

### 9. High Calls / Low Conversations (diagnostic pattern)

**Definition.** Diagnostic IF/THEN — connect rate is depressed despite high dial volume.

**What it means.** The rep is doing the activity but not producing conversations. The gap is in the quality of the calling — phone type, timing, scripting, voicemail strategy.

**Diagnostic questions.** Are they trying both cell and landline? Are they reviewing call dispositions? Are they leaving strong voicemails that point to email? Are they calling during proper hours? Are there consistent objections they're failing to handle?

**TASK level.** Skill (S) — calling technique. Sometimes Knowledge (K) — they don't know the prospect well enough to be relevant.

**Champion's Code element.** Element 3 (Coaching) on call technique; Element 4 (Practice) on roleplay.

### 10. High Conversations / Low Meetings Set (diagnostic pattern)

**Definition.** Diagnostic IF/THEN — they're getting people on the phone, but not converting to meetings.

**What it means.** The conversion mechanic between conversation and meeting commitment is broken. Usually a skill issue around asking for the meeting.

**Diagnostic questions.** Are they following the TALKER framework? Are they asking pain & impact questions? Are they asking for the meeting with a reason that matters to the prospect? Are they leveraging research and personalizing scripting?

**TASK level.** Skill (S) — meeting-ask technique.

**Champion's Code element.** Element 3 (Coaching) and Element 4 (Practice) — this is what roleplay was invented for.

## Conversion metrics

### 11. Show Rate / Hold Rate

**Definition.** Percentage of scheduled meetings that actually run.

**What it means.** Booking meetings is half the job; getting them to show is the other half. Reps often skip the confirmation discipline.

**Diagnostic questions.** How far out is the meeting booked? If more than a week, are there touch points in between? Are calendar invites accepted on the call? Are they "selling" the demo (what does the prospect get from showing up)? Are confirmation calls happening?

**TASK level.** Skill (S) — meeting-confirmation discipline.

**Champion's Code element.** Element 3 (Coaching) on the closing-the-loop micro-skill.

### 12. SAL Rate / Opportunity Qualification Rate

**Definition.** Percentage of SQLs the rep accepts into pipeline as Sales Accepted Opportunities.

**What it means.** A measurement of qualification discipline. A low rate suggests over-qualification; a high rate combined with a low close rate suggests under-qualification.

**Diagnostic questions.** Is SPICED documented for accepted opps? What's the most common DQ reason? Is the rep DQing to preserve close rate (gaming)? Are inbound leads from marketing actually qualified?

**TASK level.** Knowledge (K) for qualification criteria; Skill (S) for application.

**Champion's Code element.** Element 6 (Continuous Learning) — qualification standards evolve; Element 5 (Collaboration) — peer calibration sessions.

### 13. Win Rate / Close Rate

**Definition.** Percentage of SAOs that convert to closed-won.

**What it means.** The clearest single measurement of execution effectiveness across the full sales cycle. The metric most directly attached to revenue.

**Diagnostic questions.** Did SPICED quantify the impact, or stop at pain? Was an MPP used? Was an exec connect leveraged? Was the deal multi-threaded internally and externally? Is there a mix of products in the recommendation? Were next steps consistent and recent (less than 7 days)?

**TASK level.** Skill (S) — execution across the cycle. Sometimes Knowledge (K) — gaps in product or competitive knowledge.

**Champion's Code element.** Element 3 (Coaching) on full-cycle execution; Element 6 (Continuous Learning) for win/loss patterns.

## Deal quality metrics

### 14. Deal Size / Average Contract Value

**Definition.** Net deal value (ARR, ACV, MRR).

**What it means.** A primary lever on quota attainment. Reps with disciplined value selling produce larger deals; reps with weak value selling produce discounts and partial wins.

**Diagnostic questions.** When was price first introduced — too early (anchored low) or after diagnosis? Are they recommending the full product suite or just what was asked for? Are they pulling add-ons back to the Why and ROI? Are they discounting without a trade?

**TASK level.** Skill (S) — value selling, negotiation, and trade discipline.

**Champion's Code element.** Element 3 (Coaching) on negotiation; Element 4 (Practice) on the trade conversation specifically.

### 15. Deal Time / Sales Cycle

**Definition.** Days from opportunity creation to closed-won or closed-lost.

**What it means.** Long cycles signal weak guidance through the buying process; very short cycles often signal under-qualification (closing deals that wouldn't have closed against real competition). Compare closed-won time vs. closed-lost time — if closed-lost is equal to or longer than closed-won, the rep is hanging onto dead deals.

**Diagnostic questions.** Was there a Mutual Action Plan from the start? Did they connect each meeting to the next? Did they map stakeholders? When did the customer's "evaluation" actually begin — at first contact, or weeks of education first? For closed-lost: where did the prospect leave the process?

**TASK level.** Skill (S) — deal management and qualification discipline.

**Champion's Code element.** Element 3 (Coaching); Element 6 (Continuous Learning) — deal post-mortems are the highest-leverage continuous-improvement input.

### 16. Account Coverage / Multi-Threading

**Definition.** Number of stakeholder contacts engaged per opportunity.

**What it means.** Single-threaded deals are high-risk by definition — one contact change, one champion exit, one scope reset, and the deal is over. Multi-threading drives both close rate and deal size.

**Diagnostic questions.** Are they multi-threading from early stage, or only when the deal is at risk? Have they made level alignment (VP-to-VP, Director-to-Director)? Have they asked for introductions early? Have they suggested who else should be involved based on previous deals?

**TASK level.** Skill (S) — multi-threading is one of the canonical Key Moments.

**Champion's Code element.** Element 3 (Coaching); Element 4 (Practice) — the multi-threading conversation can be roleplayed.

## How to use the library

**In a 1:1.** When a metric flags in the dashboard review (Block 2 of the 60-minute structure), the manager pulls up the matching card and runs the diagnostic questions with the rep. The rep does the diagnostic; the manager guides. The output is a single-skill development plan.

**In a Friday Deal Review.** When a deal under review has a velocity, recency, or coverage flag, the matching card generates the inspection questions. The team learns from the inspection of one deal applied to the metric.

**In a development plan.** Pick one metric. Build the four-week plan around it. Pair the metric with the matching Champion's Code element so the cultural infrastructure supports the individual coaching.

**The "one skill at a time" rule.** Never coach to more than one metric per quarter per rep. The library has 16 cards; the manager's pick rate is one. Trying to fix five metrics simultaneously produces zero metric improvements and a burned-out rep.
