# The Sacred 60-Minute One-on-One

> Methodology reference. Loaded by `manager/one-on-one-prep`, `manager/development-plan-generator`, and any Manager Layer skill that prepares for, runs during, or follows a 1:1.

## When to use this

Whenever a manager runs a weekly 1:1 with a direct report, prepares for one, or evaluates the quality of an existing 1:1 cadence. The structure is the highest-ROI hour in a manager's week, and it is the venue where most of the Core Six work actually happens.

## The core thesis

The weekly 1:1 is the manager's most leveraged hour. Yet industry data suggests **89% of 1:1s are 30 minutes or less, and 68% lack any consistent structure**. The single highest-ROI hour in a manager's week is being wasted on status updates that could have been emails.

The 60-minute 1:1 is not a meeting length preference. It is the minimum duration required to do all six things a real coaching conversation has to do — and to do them in an order that builds psychological safety before delivering hard feedback.

This 1:1 is **sacred**. It is never cancelled, only rescheduled. It is calendared a quarter ahead. Pre-work is non-negotiable for both manager and rep. If either side comes unprepared, the meeting is rescheduled. The discipline trains the discipline.

## The six objectives of the 1:1

A 1:1 is built around six distinct purposes. A meeting that hits fewer than four of these in a given week has degraded into a status update.

1. **Provide coaching and call planning** for the seven Key Moments
2. **Manage to the rep's performance goals** (Activity + outcome metrics — see `task-coaching-diagnostic.md`)
3. **Inspect deals to refine forecast** (per-deal SPICED, the upstream input to `forecast-triangulation-method.md`)
4. **Enforce CRM hygiene** ("source of truth" discipline — pipeline cannot be fictional)
5. **Identify and remove hurdles and blockers** (the manager's job is to clear runway)
6. **Understand rep career goals** (the rep is a person, not a quota-carrier)

Note that "tell the rep what to do" is not on this list. The 1:1 is a coaching session, not a directive forum. Research consistently finds that high-performing managers schedule coaching sessions vs. running them ad-hoc, spend more deal-coaching time with reps below 75% of quota, and conduct (rather than skip) the scheduled sessions when calendar pressure builds.

## The 60-minute structure

| # | Block | Duration | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Personal check-in | 5 min | Relationship and rapport — the foundation of secure attachment |
| 2 | Performance review | 10 min | Results, activity metrics, leading indicators |
| 3 | Pipeline scrub & deal inspection | 15 min | Data validation + 2–3 key deals |
| 4 | Skill development & call coaching | 20 min | Recording review, role play, TASK diagnosis |
| 5 | Career development | 5 min | Long-term goals, growth opportunities |
| 6 | Action planning | 5 min | Specific commitments with timelines |

The order matters. Personal check-in builds the safety that lets the rep receive coaching honestly. Performance review establishes the data. Pipeline scrub keeps deals moving. Skill development is the bulk of the hour because it is the only investment that compounds. Career development reminds the rep this relationship is about more than this quarter's number. Action planning closes the loop with commitments.

## Monthly topic rotation

Without rotation, every 1:1 becomes a groundhog-day pipeline review. The skill-development block (#4) rotates monthly so every dimension of the rep's development gets dedicated attention:

| Week of month | Focus |
|---|---|
| Week 1 | Territory and account planning |
| Week 2 | Pipeline management and qualification |
| Week 3 | Skill development and call coaching |
| Week 4 | Performance review and career development |

The rotation ensures that across a single month, every rep has had explicit conversation about all four dimensions. Without rotation, urgent topics (this week's pipeline) crowd out important ones (career arc, skill gaps, territory strategy) every single week.

### Quarterly emphasis overlay

The monthly rotation operates inside a quarterly rhythm where topic emphasis shifts by month-of-quarter:

- **Month 1 of quarter:** Account and territory plan-setting; early-stage qualification rigor (M1-M2 carry the pipeline build)
- **Month 2 of quarter:** Continued early-stage focus; mid-stage advancement
- **Month 3 of quarter:** Late-stage pipeline review intensifies; closing techniques; results review against plan

The rotation is the structure. The overlay is the emphasis. A Week-2 (Pipeline) session in Month 1 looks different from a Week-2 session in Month 3 — same topic, different operational urgency.

## Pre-work requirements

The pre-work is the discipline that makes the meeting work. Without it, the 60 minutes degrades to status updates regardless of the structure on paper.

### Manager pre-work (before each 1:1)

- Review CRM, performance dashboard, and recent call recordings for this rep
- Identify 2–3 deals requiring inspection (selected for evidence gaps, stage duration violations, or strategic importance — start with newest opportunities to ensure they were qualified well)
- Prepare a coaching focus area for the session — one specific TASK-level diagnosis, not "let's talk about discovery"
- Pull last week's commitments and prepare to recap

The manager's prep block (15 minutes) goes on the calendar as a separate event immediately preceding the 1:1. Without it, the manager is improvising — and improvised coaching is closer to opinion than diagnosis.

### Rep pre-work (48 hours before)

- Update all CRM data completely
- Submit self-assessment on the focus skill
- Identify deals where they need support
- Document questions and challenges

If either side comes unprepared, the meeting reschedules. Cancelling for unpreparedness is the meta-lesson; running the meeting unprepared trains the team that the meeting doesn't matter.

## After the meeting

By the start of the next business day, the manager sends a written summary that:

- Captures agreements reached and action items with owners and dates
- Provides **specific** positive reinforcement on what the rep did well in the session
- Names the focus for the week ahead

Specificity matters. "Good job" is noise — even children stop responding to generic praise. "I was impressed by how you described the customer's pain in the BMC deal — you went a layer deeper than usual" is reinforcement. Specific recognition activates intrinsic motivation; generic recognition does not. One specific positive observation per session, every session, is the discipline.

## Cancellation protocol

The 1:1 is never cancelled, only rescheduled within the same week. Cancellation signals "this isn't important," which destroys the entire system over time.

If the manager has a true conflict, **the rep does not lose the slot — the manager owns rebooking it**. Putting the burden on the rep to chase a new slot reverses the principal-agent relationship and erodes the meeting's status.

For vacations longer than one week, the manager's manager picks up direct 1:1s. Otherwise the gap creates real coaching debt that compounds.

## Skip-level coaching

Once per quarter, the manager's manager joins each rep's 1:1 (with the manager). This:

- Validates that the manager is coaching well
- Gives the rep visibility upward
- Surfaces issues the rep may not raise to the direct manager

Skip-levels are not a bypass of the manager. They are a quality-control layer for the coaching system.

## Documentation discipline

Every 1:1 produces a shared doc with:

- Action items (owner + date)
- Coaching focus for the week
- Deals inspected
- Skill commitments

The doc is reviewed at the start of next week's 1:1. This creates the accountability loop that makes coaching stick. Coaching that does not get documented does not compound.

## Common 1:1 anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Status meeting | Rep recites their week; manager listens | Skip the status block; require pre-work to surface what would have been status |
| Pipeline-only | Every 1:1 is the same pipeline scrub | Enforce monthly topic rotation in the skill-development block |
| Manager monologue | Manager talks 60%+ of the time | Track speaking time; target ≤30% for the manager |
| Drift to 30 min | Meeting consistently runs short | Audit pre-work — short meetings usually mean unprepared meetings |
| Cancellation creep | "Just this week" cancellations stack up | Reschedule, never cancel; manager owns rebooking |
| No documentation | Action items live only in someone's head | Require shared doc; review at start of next 1:1 |

## The 1:1 as the venue for the Core Six

Most of what a manager does in the Core Six happens *inside* the 60-minute 1:1, not in some separate forum:

- **Opportunity Qualification** → block 3 (pipeline scrub & deal inspection)
- **Deal Management** → block 3 + escalations triggered in block 6
- **Call Planning & Coaching** → block 4 (skill development & call coaching)
- **Territory Management** → block 4 in Week 1 of monthly rotation
- **Account Management** → block 4 in Week 1 of monthly rotation
- **Team Skill Development** → block 4 across all weeks; aggregated into Friday Week B Skill Workshop

This is why the 1:1 is sacred. It is not "an hour for the rep" — it is the operating system through which the manager actually does the job.
