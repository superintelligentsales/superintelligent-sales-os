## Why This Document Exists

Most sales organizations inspect deals badly. The inspection cadence is wrong, the format is wrong, the frameworks are misapplied, and the result is a forecast nobody trusts and a pipeline full of fiction. This document presents a different approach — one I've refined across a decade of sales transformation work and now operationalize through the Manager Layer of the Superintelligent Sales OS.

Three claims drive everything that follows:

1. **A single forecasting method is always wrong.** The honest forecast is a band, not a number — produced by triangulating three independent methods and reconciling the gaps.
2. **Weekly forecast calls produce diminishing returns.** Stage movement takes 12-15 days. Inspecting the same deal seven days after the last inspection yields no new information about 70% of the time. The cadence belongs in 1:1s and bi-monthly reviews, not in a recurring weekly forum.
3. **Most pipeline reviews are theater.** Reps narrate progress. Managers say "sounds good." Risks hide behind happy ears. The fix is structured inspection of pre-selected deals using a qualification framework as a scalpel — what I call **Pipeline Surgery**.

The methodology that follows is built to deliver a single headline business outcome: **15-20% improvement in forecast accuracy within one quarter.**

## Part 1 · The Diagnostic Lens

Before any of this becomes operational, the manager needs the right lens. Three principles govern how to look at deals.

### Beneath metrics are behaviors

You cannot coach a number. You can only coach the activities and skills that produce it. A manager inspecting only the lagging metric — *"the forecast is short"* — is coaching the wrong thing. The right inspection drills past the number to the behavior that produced it: which deals are real, which qualification gaps are open, which buyer signals are being misread.

### Pipeline Theater vs. Pipeline Surgery

The deepest pathology in most pipeline reviews is what I call **Pipeline Theater**: the manager goes around the room asking for updates, reps provide subjective optimistic summaries, the manager says "sounds good" and moves on. Zero value added. Risks hidden by happy ears. The forecast becomes a work of fiction. Thirty minutes wasted, every week, across every team.

The alternative is **Pipeline Surgery**: an active, structured inspection of pre-selected deals using a qualification framework as a scalpel. The manager becomes a diagnostician, asking probing questions to stress-test deal health. *"What evidence do you have? How do we get to the Economic Buyer? What is our quantifiable value proposition?"* The output is a critical gap identified, a concrete action plan, and a forecast based on reality.

The empirical case is overwhelming:

- **67%** of deals in the *Proposal* stage have never had a confirmed, accessible budget
- **73%** of stalled deals are missing confirmed access to the Economic Buyer
- **89%** of deals lost to *no decision* lacked a quantified business case showing the cost of inaction

Pipeline Theater allows these statistics to kill the forecast. Pipeline Surgery prevents it.

### Three deal-killers, named explicitly

Before any forecast triangulation, every deal in the period gets inspected for three specific failure modes. These are not the only things that kill deals, but they are the three that managers most consistently miss.

| Deal-killer | Diagnostic question | What "no" looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **No critical event** | *"What happens to them if this isn't solved by [date]?"* | "Nothing material" — the deal will slip indefinitely |
| **Single-threaded** | *"Who else have we engaged at director level or above?"* | "Nobody" — the deal dies if your champion changes roles |
| **No quantified impact** | *"What's the dollar value of solving this?"* | The rep can't answer — neither can the prospect — procurement will compress price |

A deal failing any of the three is not a real opportunity. It's a hope. The right inspection labels it accordingly.

## Part 2 · The Qualification Framework: SPICED

For the majority of B2B sales motions, my preferred qualification framework is SPICED. It is more conversation-driven, more buyer-centric, and easier for reps to internalize than MEDDPICC. SPICED is what gets used in the bi-monthly forecast review and in every 1:1 deal inspection.

### The five elements

| Element | What it captures |
|---|---|
| **S — Situation** | ICP fit, current state, trigger event |
| **P — Pain** | Multiple specific pain points, prioritized by impact |
| **I — Impact** | Quantified emotional and rational consequences, ROI |
| **C — Critical Event** | Hard deadline with consequences if missed |
| **E — Decision** | Process, criteria, committee, budget |

### How SPICED scoring drives the forecast

SPICED is not a checklist. It is a scoring framework. Each element is rated 1-5 with **specific evidence required** — direct quotes from the buyer, written confirmation, documented stakeholder responses. A SPICED scorecard without evidence is a story. A SPICED scorecard with evidence is a deal.

Empirical pattern observed across client engagements:

- Deals scoring **20+ on the 1-5 scale** (across all five elements) close at roughly 70%+ probability
- Deals scoring under **12** are not deals — they are hopes. They belong in the development pipeline, not the forecast.

The score is what gets reviewed in pipeline inspection. Movement on the score is what triggers stage change. Stage change without SPICED movement is a red flag.

### When MEDDPICC is the right tool instead

SPICED is the default. MEDDPICC is appropriate when three conditions hold simultaneously:

1. **Deal complexity** — multiple stakeholders, formal decision processes, multi-year contracts
2. **Procurement involvement** — formal RFP processes, security reviews, legal redlines as default
3. **Enterprise deal sizes** — typically $100K+ ACV with 6+ month sales cycles

For complex enterprise deals matching all three, MEDDPICC's eight elements (Metrics, Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria, Decision Process, Paper Process, Implicated Pain, Champion, Competition) provide the additional structure that justifies the additional overhead.

For the vast majority of B2B SaaS sales motions, SPICED is the better tool. Most teams over-rotate on MEDDPICC because it sounds more rigorous. Sounds is not the same as is.

### Stage-specific qualification standards

Stage advancement requires manager validation, not rep self-attestation. Without exit criteria enforcement, every rep's pipeline becomes optimistic fiction.

| Stage | Minimum required to advance |
|---|---|
| Lead / SQL | ICP fit confirmed, persona engaged, one pain acknowledged, discovery scheduled |
| SAL (post-demo) | Full SPICED documented, multiple stakeholders engaged, price range confirmed |
| SQL (proposal) | Value confirmed by prospect, ROI validated, budget and timeline confirmed, competition known |

## Part 3 · The Forecast Triangulation Method

A single forecasting method is always wrong. Each method has a structural blind spot that another method covers. The honest forecast is not a number — it is a **band**, produced by triangulating three independent methods and reconciling the gaps.

Managers who report a single point estimate to leadership are either overconfident or hiding the uncertainty. Managers who report a band — with the spread named as the diagnostic — build forecast credibility over time.

### The three methods

#### Method 1 — Bottom-Up Rep Commit

Each rep submits a list of specific deals they expect to close in the period, with deal-level commitment. Sum these.

- **Strength:** Reflects the rep's customer-facing intelligence — the human signals only the rep has seen
- **Weakness:** Subject to behavioral distortion (sandbagging when comp is generous, happy ears when rep is behind)

#### Method 2 — Stage-Weighted Pipeline

Apply historical win rates to all opportunities expected to close in the period.

```
Forecast = Σ (Deal Amount × Stage-Specific Win Rate × Close-Date-In-Period Probability)
```

- **Strength:** Statistically grounded; removes individual rep bias
- **Weakness:** Treats every deal as average; doesn't reflect deal-specific signals

#### Method 3 — Historical Run-Rate

Trailing four-quarter average, adjusted for seasonality and trend.

- **Strength:** Incorporates patterns the rep doesn't see (historical macro)
- **Weakness:** Backward-looking; misses inflection points (new product, new comp plan, market shift)

### Worked example

A team with $5M quarterly quota produces:

| Method | Output |
|---|---|
| Bottom-up rep commit | $5.2M |
| Stage-weighted pipeline | $4.6M |
| Historical run-rate | $4.8M |

The triangulated forecast is the band: **$4.6M to $5.2M, with a midpoint of ~$4.85M**. The honest message to leadership: *"we expect $4.85M with $4.6M as worst case and $5.2M as best case."* The 12% spread is the genuine uncertainty.

### Reconciliation rules

| Spread between methods | Confidence | Action |
|---|---|---|
| < 10% | High | Report midpoint; minimal investigation needed |
| 10-20% | Medium | Investigate which method has the structural advantage for this period |
| > 20% | Low | The gap is the diagnostic — investigate root cause before reporting |

When methods diverge by 20%+, three pathologies are typically at work:

1. **Reps sandbagging** — rep commit < pipeline. Typical after a quota raise or in comp accelerator structures that reward exceeding commit
2. **Reps with happy ears** — rep commit > pipeline. Typical when reps are behind and inflating weak deals
3. **Stale pipeline** — pipeline > run-rate. Old deals padding the number; the next action is a pipeline scrub

### Reference benchmarks

Stage-to-close win rates (industry typical — establish your own):

| Entry Stage | Probability of Eventual Close |
|---|---|
| Prospecting | ~27% |
| Discovery | ~28% |
| Solution | ~34% |
| Proposal / Negotiate | ~46% |
| Contract | ~88% |

Note the discontinuity between Proposal and Contract. A deal that has actually been contractually engaged is in a fundamentally different state than one merely under proposal. *"In negotiation"* forecasts often miss because reps confuse activity with commitment.

Maximum days in stage (industry typical — flag deals exceeding):

| Stage | Maximum Days | Action if Exceeded |
|---|---|---|
| Prospecting | 14 | Disqualify or escalate |
| Discovery | 21 | Inspect SPICED rigorously; consider closing as no-decision |
| Solution / Demo | 30 | Validate stakeholder engagement; check critical event |
| Proposal / Negotiate | 45 | Inspect pricing, procurement engagement |
| Contract | 30 | Escalate; identify legal/security blocker |

A deal sitting longer than maximum-days-in-stage without movement is, statistically, dying.

### Establishing your baseline numbers

Without baseline data, the formulas are guesses dressed up as math. Before triangulating:

1. Pull the last 4 quarters of closed deals from CRM
2. Calculate stage-to-stage conversion rates
3. Calculate average days-in-stage for won deals
4. Segment by deal size, segment, and rep where volume permits
5. Update quarterly

If CRM data is bad enough that this exercise can't run, **that is the first project**. Forecasting on top of fictional data is theater.

## Part 4 · The Bi-Monthly Cadence

The standard practice across most B2B sales orgs is the weekly forecast call. The standard practice is wrong for most teams.

### Why weekly forecast calls fail

Three structural problems:

1. **Stage movement is slower than the inspection cadence.** A weekly forecast call inspects the same deal multiple times before any real change has occurred. Reps perform progress because they have nothing real to report.
2. **Repetition breeds storytelling.** When there's nothing factual to surface, reps narrate. The meeting becomes a debrief of customer politeness signals rather than evidence-based qualification.
3. **Time waste compounds.** A 30-minute weekly forecast call across 8 reps consumes ~26 hours of manager time per quarter on a low-information activity. That same 26 hours, redirected to coaching, generates measurable performance improvement.

The math favors coaching. Every weekly forecast call kept is roughly an hour of coaching not delivered.

### The bi-monthly structure

#### Beginning of Month — Comprehensive Review (90 minutes)

The anchor meeting of the cadence. Output is a fully triangulated forecast with confidence band.

| Activity | Duration |
|---|---|
| Pull complete pipeline view | Pre-work |
| Inspect every deal expected to close in the period | 60 min (~8 min per deal across ~10 deals) |
| Apply SPICED rigor to each | embedded |
| Establish forecast commitment for the period (Commit / Best Case / Pipeline) | 15 min |
| Identify at-risk deals and resource needs | 15 min |

**Pre-work is non-negotiable.** 24 hours before the meeting, the rep submits in CRM:

- Updated SPICED scorecard with evidence
- Stakeholder map with engagement status
- Specific next step with date
- Forecast category
- Confidence assessment with rationale

If pre-work is incomplete, the deal is not reviewed. Discipline trains the discipline.

#### Mid-Month — Adjustment Checkpoint (45 minutes)

Half the BOM duration, focused only on what has changed.

| Activity | Duration |
|---|---|
| Review changes since BOM | 15 min |
| Surface deals that have slipped or accelerated | 15 min |
| Adjust forecast based on new information | 10 min |
| Reallocate resources to highest-leverage deals | 5 min |

#### The Month-3 Problem (the defining business case)

The signature failure mode of weekly forecast cadences is the **Month-3 surprise**. Quarter month 1 looks fine. Month 2 looks fine. Month 3 reveals a $1M+ gap that was actually opening up in month 2 — but the manager was inspecting end-of-quarter commit using weekly storytelling, not evidence-based mid-month inspection, so the slip surfaced too late to recover.

The Mid-Month checkpoint is specifically designed to surface this gap **4 weeks earlier** than a weekly cadence does — when there is still time to accelerate at-risk deals, redirect resources, or set leadership expectations honestly. This single inspection moment recovers more forecast credibility than any other intervention in the manager's calendar.

### What replaces the weekly forecast call

The recovered weekly time gets reinvested in three places:

1. **Pipeline scrubs** — embedded in 1:1s. Data hygiene happens during the rep's coaching hour, not in a standalone forum.
2. **Deep Dive Deal Reviews** — collaborative strategy on 2-3 deals per week.
3. **Skill development sessions** — coaching workshops, role play, focused practice.

This is not a reduction in deal inspection. It is a reallocation of inspection from a low-leverage forum (weekly forecast call) to higher-leverage forums.

### When weekly forecast calls *are* appropriate

The bi-monthly cadence is the default. The exceptions:

| Condition | Why weekly is justified |
|---|---|
| Last month of quarter | Commit is being finalized; the inspection density matters |
| Crisis recovery | Two consecutive missed forecasts; trust is broken; weekly inspection rebuilds credibility |
| New manager (first 90 days) | Manager is establishing baseline pattern recognition; needs higher inspection frequency |
| New product/segment launch | No historical data; inspection is the only signal source |

In all four cases, the weekly cadence is temporary. The default state is bi-monthly.

## Part 5 · Three Distinct Types of Deal Reviews

One of the most important distinctions in this work, because most managers conflate all three and get the worst of all worlds.

| Type | Purpose | Question | Format |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Forecast Call** | Predict the number | *"How much will you sell this quarter?"* | 30 min, numbers only, no storytelling |
| **Pipeline Scrub** | Validate data accuracy | *"Is this data right?"* | Embedded in 1:1s, focuses on values, dates, stages |
| **Deep Dive Deal Review** | Collaborative strategy | *"How do we win this deal?"* | 60 min, 2 deals, peer + leader input |

**Warning sign:** too much storytelling on a forecast call means it has degraded into a deal review. Stop and reset.

### The Deep Dive Deal Review structure

For the 60-minute, 2-deal Deep Dive format:

1. Deal overview (5 min)
2. SPICED deep dive with evidence (15 min)
3. Win strategy: competitive position, multi-threading, timeline (10 min)
4. Action planning with owners and dates (5 min)

Repeat for second deal. Pre-work is mandatory: rep submits CRM-updated SPICED scorecard and call recordings 48 hours in advance.

### Mutual Action Plans for deals above ~$30K

For deals above ~$30K with multi-stakeholder approval, a Mutual Action Plan (MAP) is non-negotiable. A MAP is a shared timeline of milestones, owners, and dependencies — built collaboratively with the champion. It is positioned as implementation planning, not as a sales tactic. Done right, it accelerates deals 20-30% and surfaces risk early.

## Part 6 · Forecast Pathologies & How to Manage Them

Even with the right cadence and the right framework, individual reps develop forecast pathologies. The job of the manager is to detect and correct them.

| Pathology | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Hockey stick** | Massive end-of-quarter loading | Inspect early-stage rigor; build pipeline earlier |
| **Sandbagging** | Rep consistently beats forecast by 20%+ | Adjust personal probability multiplier |
| **Happy ears** | Rep consistently misses forecast | Mandatory evidence requirement for forecast inclusion |
| **Bluebirds** | Late-quarter unexpected deals | Discount appropriately; analyze for pipeline-building lessons |

Per-rep forecast accuracy should be tracked, published monthly, and trended over quarters. What gets measured gets managed; what gets published builds peer accountability.

## Part 7 · Reporting to Leadership

Every forecast report includes all three estimates. When they agree, point that out. When they don't, name the gap and the reason. This builds trust by showing your work.

### The forecast credibility curve

Forecast accuracy compounds reputationally. So does forecast inaccuracy. The trajectory of a manager's credibility with leadership tracks directly with their forecast accuracy over consecutive quarters:

- **Quarter 1:** Forecast within ±15% — earn permission to be heard
- **Quarters 2-3:** Forecast within ±10% — earn the benefit of the doubt
- **Quarter 4+:** Forecast within ±5% — earn resources, autonomy, and the next promotion

A manager who forecasts within ±10% over four quarters has, by definition, inspected deals rigorously, coached reps to qualify well, held the team accountable to evidence, and built a culture of honesty. The bi-monthly cadence is what makes all four of those possible without consuming the calendar.

## Part 8 · Common Objections (And How to Handle Them)

When introducing this methodology to a sales organization, the same four objections appear consistently. Each has a precise counter:

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| *"Leadership wants weekly numbers"* | Provide weekly numbers via dashboard (CRM-pulled, low effort). The **meeting** is bi-monthly; the **reporting** can be weekly. |
| *"How will I know if a deal is slipping?"* | The mid-month checkpoint catches it. If a deal slips between BOM and Mid-Month, the rep escalates in 1:1, not in a forecast call. |
| *"Reps need the discipline of weekly accountability"* | Weekly accountability happens in the 1:1, where it should happen. Forecast calls are not the appropriate forum for general accountability. |
| *"We always do weekly"* | "Always" is not a justification. Audit the last quarter's weekly forecast calls and ask: what new information did each one surface? If the answer is "very little 70% of the time," the data has answered the question. |

## Part 9 · Implementation Sequencing

For teams transitioning from weekly to bi-monthly inspection, the right sequencing is three months long:

1. **Month 1:** Run BOM (90 min) and Mid-Month (45 min) **in addition to** existing weekly calls. Compare information yield. The weekly calls will reveal themselves as low-yield by direct contrast.
2. **Month 2:** Drop weekly calls. Reps and manager use the recovered time for coaching and pipeline scrubs in 1:1s.
3. **Month 3:** Measure. Forecast accuracy should hold or improve. Coaching session count should rise. Reactive escalations should decrease.

If forecast accuracy degrades in Month 3, the issue is almost never the cadence — it is CRM hygiene, SPICED discipline, or a specific rep with a personal probability multiplier issue. Fix those upstream causes; do not revert the cadence.
